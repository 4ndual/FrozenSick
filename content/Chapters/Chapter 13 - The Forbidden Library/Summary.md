# Chapter 13 — The Forbidden Library

---

## Setting the Scene

The search for Dorotea and Vermilion has led Tidus, Nixira, and Zacarías into the Durst house, an isolated mansion surrounded by unnatural mist. Buster waits outside while the party explores a house whose rooms, sounds, and inhabitants refuse to behave as they should.

The Curse of Strahd arc continues inside the house. Its upper floors remain unexplored, and the warning of a beast beneath it has yet to be tested.

---

## The Story

**The Wolf and the Ward**

The party climbs the spiral staircase to a dark landing, where a suit of armor bears a black wolf's head. Nixira examines the carved double doors nearby and identifies their gray finish as ash made from human bones. The craftsmanship, like so much else in the house, seems very old.

Tidus removes the wolf's head from the armor. Zacarías kicks it and feels a blow himself, leaving a bump. The house, they learn, has a protective enchantment that can return damage to someone who damages its property. Breaking things may carry a price.

**The Servants' Room**

Two neatly made beds, chests, and a wardrobe furnish a room that appears to have belonged to servants. The chests hold a butler's uniform and a maid's uniform; the clothing suggests an age long past. Above the beds are traces of burned material, perhaps from something humanoid, though the party cannot determine what happened there.

A creak and a young girl's voice disturb the search. Zacarías finds a single polished-shoe print in the wardrobe, pointing out into the room. Later, as the party prepares to leave, the print disappears before his eyes.

Tidus puts on the butler's uniform. When the travelers return to the landing, the wolf's head is back on its armor. Tidus takes it again and wears it with his new outfit, becoming an unlikely wolf-headed butler. In the carving on the double doors, the party now sees a larger image: the landscape forms a fanged mouth.

**A Reader Without a Body**

Beyond the doors lies a great library, its floor clean despite a filthy ceiling. Rats move within the house, and beyond the windows the mist still presses close. Zacarías speaks with Buster and reassures him. The horse remains outside.

An open blue book floats over a chair as though someone unseen is reading it. When it falls, Tidus feels a cold presence pass him. The book is an old romance; Nixira also finds a key marked with the family's mill emblem and pockets it. Its lock remains unknown.

Zacarías tries to make contact using a jewel as a pendulum. A spectral hand appears briefly, and the romance opens to an illustration of a woman reading in a tower with a shadowy figure behind her. A drop falls upon a passage about her death. Whatever answered, the party cannot yet be certain of its identity or intentions.

The house's dimensions trouble Tidus. Behind the shelves, he finds an area that sounds hollow. Elsewhere, Zacarías removes a board and discovers an opaque magical barrier. It resists passage, and the group looks for a way to open it rather than risk the house's protective magic.

**The Children and the Piano**

Nixira plays her harp, and a piano answers. Following the sound, the party finds an old grand piano and a small figure identified as the boy Thorn. When Nixira plays, a jewel-like feature glows purple.

The pendulum points Zacarías toward the area between the sitting room and library. At the piano, a classical piece brings out a bloodstain that vanishes when the music stops. Tidus investigates: the blood is human, its purple color associated with corruption and dark magic.

Using Zacarías's focus, Tidus hears children's voices. They say their father forbade them from entering the dangerous room, and promise to help if the party can free them. They are frightened of leaving their room upstairs. Growls interrupt the contact.

Nixira tries to reach them in turn. The cord burns away, Zacarías is seized by pain, and she hears an unfamiliar voice declare that the visitors are unwelcome in its domain. The party does not know whose voice it is.

**Music for a Hidden Door**

The travelers turn back to the study. Its desk contains paper and book-repair supplies, but no obvious lock for Nixira's key. She repairs a damaged desk lamp with magic. When lit, it projects an incomplete pattern onto the papers; the shelves, too, have an arrangement that seems deliberate.

Together the party works through the clues. The pattern is not writing but a broken musical score. Nixira plays it while the others work in the darkness, revealing a pattern across the books. As they pull and arrange them, Zacarías recognizes a dangerous arcane key. The corrupted blood forms part of the final step, and the barrier opens.

**The Letter and the Chest**

Behind it is a forbidden library. Zacarías warns the others that its books are cursed. Their shelves contain accounts of demonic and necromantic rituals, many described as false. A separate lamp reveals sinister markings hidden from ordinary sight.

A skeleton in leather armor rests near a heavy chest, a letter clutched in its hand. The signs suggest an intruder killed by a dart trap after entering through the ceiling; the trap's mechanism is now broken. The party takes the letter outside the room and reads it.

Its author, Strahd von Zarovich, rejects the addressee's belief that he is a messiah or a guide to immortality. He accuses the addressee and associates of sacrifice at a hidden altar and the torture of visitors in a dungeon, and refers to a child born dead. These are the letter's claims; the party has not witnessed the acts it describes.

The chest also holds three blank leather-bound books, three spell scrolls, and official papers: deeds to the house and a mill, and a will. The will leaves family property to Rose and Thorn if their parents die. The mill is described as lying east of Vallaki, a place unfamiliar to the travelers. The party discusses the implications, but no property passes to them here.

They consider going upstairs and freeing the children. For now the search pauses: the spirits remain unreleased, the sisters remain missing, and the rest of the house waits.

## Key Revelations

- **The house protects its property** — Damage to an object can be reflected back at the person responsible.
- **The children want help** — Voices Tidus hears say they are afraid to leave their room and offer help if freed. The party has not yet freed them.
- **Music opens the way** — A repaired lamp, a hidden score, the books, and corrupted blood lead the party through the magical barrier.
- **A forbidden library lies behind the wall** — Its books are cursed, and many of the rituals described in them are false.
- **Strahd's letter condemns the addressee** — It alleges torture and sacrifice, rejects a messianic role, and mentions a stillborn child. Its accusations remain to be investigated.
- **The children are named as heirs** — The will conditionally leaves them the house, mill, and other family property. The party's speculation about inheriting it is not a transfer of ownership.

---

## Consequences

- **The barrier is open** — The party has entered the hidden library and recovered its documents and contents.
- **The children remain upstairs** — Their offer of help is conditional on being freed; the party has not yet reached their room.
- **The house still holds dangers** — The cursed books, the unknown voice, and the warning about what lies beneath remain unresolved.
- **The search continues** — Neither Dorotea nor Vermilion is found in this session.
- **The party has new resources** — Blank books and spell scrolls join the recovered papers; the scrolls' final distribution remains undecided.
